# Type 2 diabetes

Type 2 diabetes is a common health condition that can cause high blood sugar.

## What is blood sugar?

Blood sugar, or glucose, is the main type of sugar in blood. The body uses it for energy. The pancreas, a small body part behind the stomach, makes a hormone called insulin.

Insulin works like a key, letting blood sugar move from blood into the body’s cells to be used for energy.

## Why does blood sugar get too high?

With type 2 diabetes, this system doesn’t work as well as it should. This happens in 2 ways. Either the pancreas doesn’t make enough insulin, or the insulin made doesn’t work correctly.

Both of these means sugar stays in the blood instead of going into cells for energy.

## Symptoms of type 2 diabetes

People with type 2 diabetes can have no symptoms for a long time. The signs often appear slowly over a few years, so some people don’t realise they have it. It’s often found during a check-up.

Common symptoms include:

- feeling very [thirsty](https://www.nhsinform.scot/illnesses-and-conditions/nutritional/thirst/)
- needing to pee more than usual, especially at night
- feeling very tired
- losing weight without trying
- cuts or ulcers that take a long time to heal
- blurred vision
- repeatedly getting [thrush](https://www.nhsinform.scot/illnesses-and-conditions/sexual-and-reproductive/thrush/)

If any of these signs appear, it is important to see a healthcare professional as soon as possible.

## Causes of type 2 diabetes

Anyone can get type 2 diabetes, but some things make it more likely.

### Risk factors that can’t be changed

There are some risk factors that you can’t change that make you more likely to have type 2 diabetes. These include your:

- age
- family history
- family background

It’s more common as people get older.

If a close relative has type 2 diabetes, you’re more likely to develop it.

People from certain family backgrounds are more at risk from a younger age, including:

- South Asian
- Chinese
- African-Caribbean
- Black African

### Risk factors that can be changed

There are some risk factors that you can change that make you more likely to have type 2 diabetes. These include:

- your weight
- your lifestyle

Being overweight is a common risk factor, especially if weight is carried around the tummy.

## Symptoms of diabetes

The most common symptoms of diabetes are:

- feeling thirsty all the time
- peeing more than usual
- feeling very tired
- losing weight without trying

## Urgent advice: Ask for an urgent GP appointment or get help from NHS 111 if:

- you or your child has any symptoms of diabetes

You can call 111 or [get help from 111 online](https://111.nhs.uk/). Call 111 if you're asking about a child under 5 years old.

## Non-urgent advice: See a GP if you or your child:

- have a higher risk of developing diabetes

Not everyone with diabetes has symptoms, so it's important to get checked out.

## Causes of diabetes

Diabetes is caused by your body not making enough or any of a hormone called insulin, or the insulin not working properly (insulin resistance).

Insulin helps your body use glucose (sugar) for energy. Without it, the level of glucose in your blood becomes too high.

It's not possible to prevent type 1 diabetes because it's usually caused by a problem with your immune system (autoimmune condition).

You may be more likely to get type 2 or gestational diabetes because of your weight, age, ethnicity, or if other people in your family have it.
